# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Julian for Frontend Developer Lead roles (and full-time positions generally). They land here from a CV, LinkedIn, or job application link, skim quickly to confirm seniority and fit, then decide whether to reach out or move to the next candidate. They are not deeply technical evaluators on this page — they're pattern-matching for credibility, trajectory, and personality fit within seconds.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that functions as an extended, living resume: it proves Julian's frontend leadership trajectory (Junior to Team Lead in 4 years), technical range (React, Vue, Next, Nuxt, WordPress, Shopify), and public-facing credibility (conference talks, workshops, competition judging). Success = a recruiter feels confident enough in competence and character to book a call or forward the profile internally.

## Brand Personality

Warm and personal. Not corporate-stiff, not startup-hype. The site should read like a capable, friendly team lead you'd actually want on your team, not a resume PDF trying to impress. Confidence should come through competence and specifics (real talks, real metrics, real projects), not bravado.

## Anti-references

- Generic templated portfolio sites (stock "developer portrait in front of monitor" photos, cookie-cutter Bootstrap/Wix portfolio kits).
- Cold enterprise-corporate tone (stiff headshots, navy suit vibes, stock-photo handshakes).
- Overly flashy startup-SaaS cliches (hero metric tiles, gradient-text everywhere, glass cards) beyond what's already deliberately used.

## Design Principles

- Show, don't tell: every claim (leadership, speaking, mentoring) should be backed by a specific, named instance, not just an adjective.
- Warmth through specificity: a real, approachable photo and concrete details beat abstract icons when it comes to building trust with a recruiter meeting Julian for the first time.
- Confidence without noise: the existing bold typography and color accents already carry "confident" — new elements (like photography) should carry "personal" and "trustworthy" rather than compete for the same attention.
- Recruiter-first skimmability: anything added must make the page easier to size up in seconds, not just decorate it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA expectations: sufficient text contrast, meaningful alt text on any imagery added, no motion-only affordances, respect reduced-motion preference.
